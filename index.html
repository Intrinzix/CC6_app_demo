Second Life Studio
How it works
  1. The student describes the item and ticks a short self-check. The app shows the credits on offer.
  2. The app issues a QR tag. The student attaches it and drops the item at North Spine, South Spine or a hall bin.
  3. A volunteer scans the tag at the desk and compares the item with the student's grade. This takes seconds, so nobody sorts a pile by hand.
  4. Credits are released only after that check. Good items go on the shelf, damaged ones to upcycling, rejects to a partner such as Cloop.
  5. Students reserve shelf items with credits, or pay the cash price at the booth.
Credit rules (proposed)

One table sets both what a donation earns and what the same item costs on the shelf. Condition sets the base. A material factor lowers it for blends and synthetics, which resell poorly and cannot be recycled yet. Results round up, with a minimum of 1.

  • Donation credits are capped at per semester, so donating cannot become a way to fund more buying. Items over the cap are still accepted, but earn nothing.
  • 1 credit = S$1 at the booth, so students with little to donate can still pay cash.
  • Volunteers earn credits per hour. These sit outside the cap, and no shifts run in exam weeks.
  • Reserved items must be collected within 3 days, or the credits return and the item goes back on the shelf.
Under the hood (proposed design)
  • Platform: mobile web app opened from the QR poster, no install. Sign-in through NTU single sign-on, so each wallet maps to one student.
  • QR tag: carries only the donor's claim, in the form SLS|tag|item|grade|fabric. Credits are decided on the server when the volunteer confirms, so a copied or edited tag cannot mint credits.
  • Data kept: tag code, item type, fabric, grade, status, timestamps and a pseudonymous wallet ID. No photos or names.
  • Abuse controls: one-time tags, a credit cap, credits only after a check, and rejects earn nothing.
